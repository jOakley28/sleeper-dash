How the numbers are calculated

1. Each player’s expected points

Start with Sleeper’s projection, scored with your league’s rules and scaled by position to match how your league’s starters really scored. Then blend in the player’s history. The mix below is fitted from your league’s past games, not set by hand.

2. Game environment

ESPN’s betting lines (each game’s total and spread) show how many points a team is expected to score. The page checks whether they improve predictions on past weeks and uses them only if they do.

3. Who is actually playing

  • A player who has already scored in a game is treated as playing.
  • Out, Suspended, PUP: 0 points. On a bye: no projection.
  • Out mid-game: the live score keeps what they scored, and the pre-game table keeps their original projection.
  • Fewer than 3 games of history: the projection alone, with a 25% wider spread.
  • IR but projected: counted at 75%. Questionable or Doubtful: 50%.
The last two percentages and the 25% are my assumptions, not learned from your data. Injury status comes from Sleeper when the page loads. Bench players are never counted.

4. Uncertainty

Each player’s spread comes from how far real scores landed from projections in past weeks, by projection size. It shrinks as their game ends, and it is skewed, so remaining points can’t go below zero.

5. Win chance

The page simulates the rest of every starter’s game 5,000 times and counts how often each team finishes ahead. Odds stay between 1% and 99% while either team has time left, and reach 100% only once a team is ahead and its opponent has no time left. During games it also tests pace-aware versions, where a player’s scoring so far nudges his rest-of-game forecast, and it keeps whichever version scores better.
